One red, ten stops.

A design system colour ramp, before and after. Six reds that all started at the fill end became ten stops, each read against the two on-colours the app uses: white and #1B1B1B.

01 / Problem statement

A palette with badges is not a ramp

I lead the design system for a trading app. Investors read these colours every day. Designers build from them, and so does Claude, through the component library.

Role: design system lead. Scope: colour primitives, seven ramps. Error carries the story here.

Version 2.0 put contrast badges on every swatch and marked no answer. A designer picking a red for a button, a banner or a dark screen had to guess which stop was safe. The ramp also stopped at six stops that all started at the fill end.

02 / Constraints

Four rules the ramp had to meet

  1. Two pages. Every colour has to read on white and on #1B1B1B.
  2. One fill for both modes. A fill with a white label needs 4.5:1 on the label and 3:1 against each page. That pins its relative luminance to 0.133 to 0.183.
  3. Brand colours are fixed. Blue #0043CE and orange #F78F20 were already set, so Error had to fit around them.
  4. Steps you can tell apart. Under 1.2:1 between neighbours and two stops read as one.
